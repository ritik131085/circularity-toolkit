Core principle

Do not start with the facility.

Start with the material, catchment, collection behaviour, downstream market and institutional ownership. Then size infrastructure around the system you can actually operate.

Implementation pathway

Six decisions turn intent into an operating system.

The sequence is practical: understand the material first, then design infrastructure, economics and operating discipline around it.

System playbooks

Different contexts need different circularity models.

Choose the architecture that fits the waste source, collection control and downstream market.

01 · Urban ward / city

Distributed collection + ward recovery

Best where dry-waste sorting must integrate tightly with municipal collection.

Critical success factor
Reliable feeder tonnage
Primary risk
Facility built before collection stabilises
Watch metric
Inbound vs design capacity
02 · Institution / campus

Controlled-source circularity hub

Best where the institution can influence source segregation, contracts and traceability.

Critical success factor
Source segregation
Primary risk
Over-sizing and low utilization
Watch metric
Recovery value / inbound tonne
03 · Regional aggregation

Hub-and-spoke recovery network

Best where feeder systems can aggregate or pre-sort before a larger secondary sorting hub.

Critical success factor
Feedstock contracts
Primary risk
Transport cost destroys value
Watch metric
Net value after logistics

Throughput

Recovery

Quality

04

Value

Is the system increasing value captured rather than only moving volume?

Net realization / inbound tonne
05

Reliability

Are equipment uptime, staffing and offtake stable?

Uptime + sale cycle time
06

Inclusion

Are livelihoods, safety and formal participation improving as the system scales?

Worker income + safety indicators
