Toolkit scope

Integrated decision support across the material recovery value chain

The toolkit brings system design, operating performance, governance, market development and economics into a single decision framework. It is intended to support decisions from early-stage system design through operational improvement and replication.

Scope of the toolkit

System guidance, decision tools, diagnostics and implementation resources across the material recovery value chain.

Decisions supported

How to structure a new system, strengthen an operating facility and scale a proven operating model.

Recommended application

Begin with the decision at hand. Use the relevant diagnostic, then move to the corresponding guidance, decision tool or operating template.

Operating framework

Guidance across ten critical dimensions of system performance

Each module sets out the decisions, operating requirements and performance indicators relevant to that part of the material recovery system.

Regulatory requirementEvidence-based operating practicePlanning assumption / screening threshold
01Context & governanceOwnership · registration · roles · incentives+

Key decisions

  • Who owns the site, assets and material value?
  • Who controls collection and feedstock?
  • Who carries residue, market and payment risk?
  • What happens when tonnage, quality or payments fall below plan?

Operating indicators

  • One accountable owner for each system function
  • Clear feedstock, payment and performance commitments
  • Risk allocation follows decision control
02Feedstock & collectionSource quality · segregation · routing · intake+

Key decisions

  • Which sources drive dry-waste volume and quality?
  • Where does contamination enter the chain?
  • How variable are volumes by route, weekday and season?
  • What grading and feedback rules apply at intake?

Operating indicators

  • Source-wise tonnage and quality data
  • No remixing after segregation
  • Intake grading linked to operating action
03Facility & infrastructureCapacity · layout · circulation · storage · weather resilience+

Key decisions

  • Secured inbound TPD versus installed capacity
  • Space for intake, sorting, storage, utilities and movement
  • Drainage, ventilation, fire safety and vehicle access

Operating indicators

  • Linear flow from intake to dispatch
  • Covered storage protects material quality
  • Mechanisation matches feedstock, skills and maintenance capability
04Workforce & inclusionRoles · training · safety · retention · formal participation+

Key decisions

  • Role-wise staffing by shift and throughput
  • Critical single-person dependencies
  • Training, payment, safety and grievance systems

Operating indicators

  • Cross-trained backup for critical roles
  • Visible safety and welfare basics
  • Waste-worker inclusion designed into the operating model
05Processing & technologyWorkflow · mechanisation · maintenance · uptime+

Key decisions

  • Where the line actually bottlenecks
  • Manual versus semi-mechanised processing
  • Preventive maintenance and critical spares

Operating indicators

  • Sequential, understandable workflow
  • Downtime tracked by cause
  • Technology solves a defined material-flow problem
06Quality & traceabilitySorting standards · storage · bale tags · special-care waste+

Key decisions

  • Accepted contamination and grade standards
  • Storage, FIFO and batch traceability
  • Isolation of sanitary, special-care or hazardous material

Operating indicators

  • Visual sorting references
  • Traceable dispatch batches
  • Buyer feedback closes back into training and source feedback
07Markets & offtakeBuyer requirements · inventory · dispatch · price discovery+

Key decisions

  • Priority materials by value and inventory risk
  • Buyer diversity, freight and payment terms
  • Dispatch frequency and working-capital exposure

Operating indicators

  • Material moves quickly into stable downstream channels
  • Buyer concentration is actively managed
  • Price and rejection are reviewed by material and buyer
08Economics & viabilityRevenue stack · unit cost · working capital · service value+

Key decisions

  • Service revenue versus material revenue
  • Cost per inbound and recovered tonne
  • Cash tied up in inventory and receivables

Operating indicators

  • Hidden subsidies are visible
  • Breakeven levers are understood
  • Scale decisions use real unit economics
09Health, risk & complianceKPIs · reporting · registered channels · early warning+

Key decisions

  • Which KPIs are reviewed monthly and who owns action?
  • What triggers escalation?
  • Can intake, output, rejects and dispatch be reconciled?

Operating indicators

  • KPIs drive action, not reporting alone
  • Near misses and data anomalies are logged
  • Required records are current and retrievable
10Scale & replicationReadiness · feedstock · value addition · geography · financing+

Key decisions

  • Whether growth should come from feedstock, capacity, material value, services or geography
  • Whether the base operation is stable enough to absorb complexity
  • What contracts, data and financing are needed before the next step

Operating indicators

  • Baseline KPIs are stable before expansion
  • SOPs and data definitions are replicable
  • Scale strengthens system economics or service performance
